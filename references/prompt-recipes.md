# Prompt Recipes

Use these recipes as construction patterns. Replace bracketed fields internally before use; never leave placeholders in a user-facing prompt.

## Integrated single-image edit

Edit the supplied photograph into a restrained abstract negative-space landscape artwork. Retain only [one precisely identified subject] from the original and preserve its recognizable silhouette, orientation, defining structure, key source colors, and [essential reflection or connected formation if applicable]. Dissolve and remove the remaining environment. Place the retained subject optically small in a large seamless [warm ivory / pearl gray / mist gray / very pale cyan] field with approximately 70-80% calm negative space; use [vertical / low-horizontal / narrow-horizontal] composition at [appropriate scale and placement]. Give the subject a subtle [chosen material family] interpretation while keeping it immediately identifiable as the original subject. Use large diffused museum light from [direction], low restrained contrast, delicate internal tonal depth, and a faint wide floating shadow with no visible floor or base. The result should feel like a landscape distilled into a poetic gallery artifact, elegant, quiet, airy, and materially believable. Preserve the original aspect ratio and do not add torn paper, paper edges, collage, scrapbook layers, frames, borders, text, labels, logos, watermarks, decorative symbols, unrelated objects, multiple subjects, hard shadows, pedestals, tabletops, room corners, strong gradients, glossy product-ad styling, or a flat pasted cutout appearance.

## Integrated batch-series instruction

Transform every supplied source photograph into one image in a coherent abstract negative-space landscape series. For each photograph, independently select and retain exactly one most recognizable subject, preserving its silhouette, orientation, defining structural features, key source color cue, and any essential attached reflection or formation; dissolve nearly all surrounding scenery. Across the entire set, lock the same [background hue and temperature], soft [light direction], low restrained contrast, approximately 70-80% negative-space balance, subtle floating-shadow softness, balanced abstraction level, and compatible [material family] vocabulary. Adapt placement to subject geometry: vertical landmarks remain small at roughly 18-25% of canvas height and slightly below center; ridges or shorelines become low horizontal forms at roughly 35-55% of canvas width and under 20% height; cloud-light bands remain slim and elongated. Keep every source identifiable and do not replace, merge, duplicate, or redesign its subject. Create quiet gallery-grade landscape specimens, not a collage, poster set, product catalog, or stock cutout series. Do not add torn paper, frames, borders, text, captions, labels, logos, watermarks, props, platforms, visible floors, hard shadows, unrelated scenery, or inconsistent background colors.

## Landmark and reflection

Retain the original landmark together with its complete reflection as one unified vertical subject. Preserve the landmark's exact orientation, defining silhouette, main structural features, and signature color. Reduce it to roughly 18-25% of canvas height, place it slightly off-center and below the midpoint, and surround it with a seamless warm pearl-gray field containing about 75% breathing room. Let the reflection transition gently into translucent resin or frosted glass without detaching, duplicating, flipping incorrectly, or cropping it. Use soft top-left museum light and one faint wide floating shadow. Remove all other landscape context. Keep the scene poetic and recognizable, never like a pasted cutout or retail object.

## Salt ridge or shoreline

Retain one continuous, distinctive ridge or shoreline segment from the original photograph and preserve its natural contour, mineral rhythm, and original relationship between pale deposits and shallow water. Reform it as a low horizontal landscape specimen spanning roughly 35-55% of the canvas width and no more than 20% of its height. Surround it with a seamless pearl-gray to very pale cyan field and about 75% quiet negative space. Use restrained salt-crystal and translucent-water material cues, fine natural irregularity, diffused overhead light, and a nearly imperceptible floating shadow. Remove the rest of the horizon and environment. Do not turn it into jewelry, an island diorama, a product platform, or a paper collage.

## Cloud and internal light

Retain one narrow cloud layer and its original internal opening of light. Preserve the cloud's elongated contour, dark-gray tonal mass, and the location and hue of the light seam. Suspend it alone as a slim horizontal form occupying roughly 40-60% of canvas width against a seamless warm ivory or pearl-gray field with broad negative space. Interpret the cloud as soft smoke crystal or semi-opaque mineral vapor, with restrained amber light embedded within it, gentle diffused illumination, and a faint floating shadow. Remove the wider sky and all other scenery. Avoid storm spectacle, fantasy portals, neon glow, dramatic rays, glossy product rendering, or torn-paper openings.

## Corrections

### Subject too large or whitespace too weak

Keep the subject design unchanged, reduce its optical size by about 30-40%, reposition it slightly below the canvas midpoint, and expand the seamless quiet background until approximately 75-80% of the image reads as negative space. Do not add any new objects or decorative texture.

### Output became torn paper or collage

Remove all paper fibers, ripped edges, pasted layers, borders, frames, and cutout seams. Replace them with one continuous seamless luminous field. Preserve the subject as a materially coherent floating landscape specimen with soft volumetric edges, not a paper aperture or collage element.

### Subject is distorted or no longer recognizable

Restore the source subject's exact silhouette, orientation, defining structure, signature color cue, and essential reflection or connected formation. Reduce material stylization until the source is identifiable at first glance. Do not invent, duplicate, symmetrize, or replace features.

### Output looks like ordinary product photography

Remove the pedestal, tabletop, showroom floor, hard contact shadow, spotlight cone, glossy advertising highlights, and centered catalog framing. Make the subject feel like a fragment of landscape suspended in luminous space, with restrained museum light, poetic asymmetry, and a faint broad shadow.

### Series is inconsistent

Keep the individual subjects unchanged while harmonizing background color temperature, light direction, contrast, abstraction intensity, shadow softness, and material vocabulary across all outputs. Preserve geometry-aware subject placement rather than forcing identical coordinates.
